    nifreeze.analysis.sensitivity module¶

    Sensitivity of model predictions to motion of their training volumes.

    Two questions are asked of a model, on an aligned dataset:

    • fidelity(): how well does it predict each held-out volume?

    • sensitivity(): how far do its predictions drift from those on the aligned dataset when some volumes move by known rigid motions, or are distorted as by eddy currents (a perturbation)?

    A fresh model is built on each perturbed copy. A held-out prediction never uses its own volume, so every volume can be evaluated, the moved ones included: a moved volume’s prediction sees the motion of the others only. Eddy-current distortions follow the diffusion gradient, so a prediction may still resemble its volume’s own distortion through volumes with similar gradients: inherited_distortion() measures how much. Registration, the estimator and its intensity window are not involved.

    Displacements along the phase-encoding axis, polynomial in space, one per volume.

    Volume i shows at each point what lies \(d_i\) mm further along the phase-encoding axis, with its intensity scaled by the Jacobian \(1 + \partial d_i / \partial x_{PE}\) so that no signal is created or lost. \(d_i\) is a polynomial of the voxel-axis coordinates, centered on the mask and divided by its half-extent along each axis.

    Draw distortions that follow the diffusion gradients, as eddy currents do.

    The fields that a diffusion gradient leaves behind scale with its amplitude and change sign with its direction. Each volume’s coefficients are therefore one fixed random linear map of its gradient vector, scaled by \(\sqrt{b / b_{max}}\) (the gradient amplitude, at fixed timing). b=0 volumes are not distorted, and antipodal gradients get opposite distortions.

    • gradients (ArrayLike, optional) – Gradient table, (n_volumes, 4) with the b-value last; the dataset’s if None.

    • mask (ndarray, optional) – Voxels over which the polynomial is normalized and magnitude reached.

    nifreeze.analysis.sensitivity.inherited_distortion(aligned: ndarray, perturbed: ndarray, distortion: Distortion, affine: ndarray, mask: ndarray) → ndarray[source]¶

    Distortion coefficients that best explain how a prediction changed.

    To first order, displacing an image \(p\) by \(d\) along the phase-encoding axis (with its Jacobian) changes it by \(\partial_{PE} p \, d + p \, \partial_{PE} d\), which is linear in the coefficients of \(d\). Least squares over the mask give the coefficients of the distortion that the change resembles; whatever the distortion cannot explain (noise) stays in the residual. A rigid motion does not: a translation along phase encoding is the distortion’s constant term, so aligned must carry the same motions as perturbed.

    Parameters:

    • aligned (ndarray) – The prediction without the distortion.

    • perturbed (ndarray) – The prediction with the distortion.

    • distortion (Distortion) – The distortion, whose terms are fitted.

    • affine (ndarray) – Grid of the predictions.

    • mask (ndarray) – Voxels fitted.

    Returns:

    The fitted coefficients, comparable to a row of Distortion.coefficients.

    Return type:

    ndarray

    nifreeze.analysis.sensitivity.perturb(dataset: Any, perturbation: Perturbation, order: int = 3, round_trip: bool = False, grid: Any = None) → Any[source]¶

    Copy a dataset with some of its volumes moved or distorted.

    Each moved or distorted volume is resampled once from the aligned original, through its motion and then its distortion. Everything else (gradients, b=0 reference, mask) is left as it was, as a model would see it when the motion and the distortion go uncorrected.

    Parameters:

    • dataset (BaseDataset) – The aligned dataset.

    • perturbation (Perturbation) – The volumes to move, their motions, and the distortion.

    • order (int, optional) – Interpolation order.

    • round_trip (bool, optional) – Move each volume and move it back, which leaves it in place but interpolated twice (rigid motions only).

    • grid (BaseDataset, optional) – The dataset on a shifted grid (see shift_grid()). Moved volumes are then resampled from the original onto that grid, through their motion, and everything else is taken from grid.

    Returns:

    A copy of the dataset (or of grid) with float32 volumes.

    Return type:

    BaseDataset

    nifreeze.analysis.sensitivity.perturbation_plan(n_volumes: int, magnitudes: Sequence[float], counts: Sequence[int], seeds: int | Sequence[int] = 5, center: Buffer | _SupportsArray[dtype[Any]] | _NestedSequence[_SupportsArray[dtype[Any]]] | complex | bytes | str | _NestedSequence[complex | bytes | str] = (0.0, 0.0, 0.0), fixed: Iterable[int] = ()) → list[Perturbation][source]¶

    Draw the perturbations of a sensitivity experiment.

    For each seed, the moved volumes are the first count of a random ordering of the volumes not listed in fixed, each with a random rotation axis and translation direction. Volumes moved with a smaller count are thus also moved with a larger one, and they follow the same axis and direction at every magnitude, so the curves over magnitude and count are paired.

    Parameters:

    • n_volumes (int) – Number of volumes in the dataset.

    • magnitudes (Sequence) – Motion magnitudes (degrees and mm, see rigid_motion()).

    • counts (Sequence) – Numbers of moved volumes.

    • seeds (int or Sequence, optional) – Number of seeds (0, 1, ...), or the seeds themselves.

    • center (ArrayLike, optional) – World coordinates of a point on every rotation axis (see mask_centroid()).

    • fixed (Iterable, optional) – Volumes that never move.

    Returns:

    One perturbation per seed, count and magnitude.

    Return type:

    list of Perturbation

    Examples

    >>> plan = perturbation_plan(10, magnitudes=[0.5, 1.0], counts=[1, 3], seeds=2, fixed=[0])
>>> len(plan)
8
>>> any(0 in p.moved for p in plan)
False
>>> set(plan[0].moved) <= set(plan[2].moved)

    nifreeze.analysis.sensitivity.sensitivity(model: str | Callable[[...], Any], dataset: Any, plan: Iterable[Perturbation], evaluate: Iterable[int] | None = None, mask: ndarray | None = None, metrics: Mapping[str, Callable[[...], float]] | None = None, order: int = 3, grid_shift: float | Buffer | _SupportsArray[dtype[Any]] | _NestedSequence[_SupportsArray[dtype[Any]]] | complex | bytes | str | _NestedSequence[complex | bytes | str] | None = 0.25, **model_kwargs: Any) → list[dict[str, Any]][source]¶

    Measure how far held-out predictions drift from the aligned ones under a plan.

    Each prediction on a perturbed copy is compared with the prediction of the same volume on the aligned dataset. The change is reported as an NRMSE divided by the standard deviation of the actual volume, not by that of the aligned prediction: a smooth prediction would otherwise inflate its own error, and models could not be compared. By default, the whole dataset is first resampled onto a shifted grid (see shift_grid()), so that every volume is interpolated once and moved volumes differ from the others only by their displacement.

    Parameters:

    • model (str or Callable) – The model (see held_out_predictions()).

    • dataset (BaseDataset) – The aligned dataset.

    • plan (Iterable of Perturbation) – The perturbations (see perturbation_plan()).

    • evaluate (Iterable, optional) – Volumes to predict (all if None), moved or not.

    • mask (ndarray, optional) – Voxels compared, on the grid of the compared volumes (the shifted grid, unless grid_shift is None); the brain mask on that grid, or the whole volume, if None.

    • metrics (Mapping, optional) – Further metrics by name, with the signature of masked_cc(), called as metric(perturbed, aligned, mask).

    • order (int, optional) – Interpolation order.

    • grid_shift (float or ArrayLike, optional) – Offset of the shifted grid, in voxels; None resamples only the moved volumes, which then come out smoother than the others.

    • **model_kwargs – Arguments for the model.

    Returns:

    One row per perturbation and evaluated volume, with the nrmse, each further metric, whether that volume was itself moved, the magnitude of the distortion (0 without one) and the share of the volume’s own distortion that its prediction inherited (the projection of the coefficients fitted by inherited_distortion() onto the volume’s own; NaN without a distortion, or for an undistorted volume). With motions too, the share is fitted to the change from the prediction with the same motions and no distortion, since a motion alone also projects onto the distortion’s terms.

    Return type:

    list of dict

    nifreeze.analysis.sensitivity.shift_grid(dataset: Any, shift: float | Buffer | _SupportsArray[dtype[Any]] | _NestedSequence[_SupportsArray[dtype[Any]]] | complex | bytes | str | _NestedSequence[complex | bytes | str] = 0.25, order: int = 3) → Any[source]¶

    Resample a whole dataset onto a grid offset by a fraction of a voxel.

    Resampling smooths noise, which real motion does not. On the shifted grid every volume goes through one interpolation, moved or not (see perturb()), so moved and unmoved volumes are smoothed alike. Only the sampling grid moves: the anatomy stays in place. A quarter of a voxel along the diagonal smooths white noise as much as resampling through a rigid motion of 0.5 to 4 degrees and millimeters (within about 3%), whereas half a voxel smooths noticeably more.

    Parameters:

    • dataset (BaseDataset) – The aligned dataset.

    • shift (float or ArrayLike, optional) – Offset of the grid, in voxels, along each axis.

    • order (int, optional) – Interpolation order.

    Returns:

    A copy with float32 volumes on the shifted grid. The brain mask and the b=0 reference, if any, are resampled too.

    Return type:

    BaseDataset
