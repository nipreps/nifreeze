    nifreeze.registration.gem module¶

    A global, physics-based model of the eddy-current fields of a DWI (nipreps/nifreeze#745).

    A field of eddy currents linear in position moves points only along the phase-encoding direction \(u\): \(E = I - u s^T\), with three slopes \(s\) (see split_phase_encoding()). The field comes from the diffusion gradient, so its slopes follow it. In volume \(v\), with the unit b-vector \(g_v\) and the b-value \(b_v\),

    \[s_v = \left(\frac{b_v}{b_\text{ref}}\right)^p C g_v,\]

    where the tensor \(C\), which maps the gradient’s direction to the field’s slopes, and the exponent \(p\) are the same for every volume (EddyTensor). The exponent is 1/2 when the field follows the gradient’s strength at a fixed timing, and 1 when it follows the b-value, as in the phantoms that TRXScan makes (PennLINC/TRXScan#27). Alternatively, each segment of b-values has its own tensor, with the exponent held (SegmentedEddyTensor). The model can also be fitted on the volumes of a range of b-values only, and give every volume its field (fit_range of GEM). A stretch that every volume shares has no parameter to go into, as long as the b-vectors average out; fitted on images, where volumes weigh a little differently in the metric, a small part of it still reaches the fields.

    The fields are zero at one point, by default the centre of the field of view (field_of_view_centre()). Only the tensor and the exponent are estimated: each volume’s slopes follow from them, and so does its shift along phase encoding, the field’s displacement away from that point. The shift is head motion by the convention of split_phase_encoding(); each volume keeps the rest of the head motion it is given.

    Estimating the model is one registration of all the volumes at once, with one set of parameters, in which each volume moves by its own field. ITK’s transforms map a point without knowing which volume it belongs to, so GEM has ITK’s metric evaluate each volume’s similarity and its derivative, and scipy estimates the parameters.

    FSL eddy’s second-level model (--slm linear) is the antecedent: it replaces each volume’s estimated field coefficients by their least-squares fit on \((b_v / 1000)\, g_v\), which is this model with \(p = 1\), fitted to the estimates of the volumes registered one by one (EddyTensor.from_slopes() does the same, with the exponent free). Here, the model is fitted on the images; the zero point is given, whereas eddy regresses the field’s offset on the gradients; and each segment of b-values may have its own tensor.

    class nifreeze.registration.gem.EddyTensor(self, matrix: 'Any', exponent=0.5, b_ref=1000.0) → None[source]¶

    Bases: object

    The eddy-current tensor, which gives every volume its field from its b-vector and b-value.

    The tensor \(C\) maps the unit b-vector to the field’s slopes at the reference b-value; with the scanner’s frame rotated by \(R\), it becomes \(R C R^T\). In volume \(v\), the field’s slopes are \(s_v = (b_v / b_\text{ref})^p C g_v\), with \(g_v\) the b-vector as acquired (see The b-vectors), and the field is \(E_v = I - u s_v^T\).

    Examples

    >>> tensor = EddyTensor(0.02 * np.eye(3), exponent=0.5, b_ref=1000.0)
>>> gradients = [[1.0, 0.0, 0.0, 1000.0], [0.0, -1.0, 0.0, 4000.0]]
>>> tensor.slopes(gradients).round(6).tolist()
[[0.02, 0.0, 0.0], [0.0, -0.04, 0.0]]
>>> tensor.fields(gradients, [0.0, 1.0, 0.0])[1, :3, :3].round(6).tolist()
[[1.0, 0.0, 0.0], [0.0, 1.04, 0.0], [0.0, 0.0, 1.0]]

    class nifreeze.registration.gem.GEM(self, align_config: 'Mapping[str, Any] | str | PathLike | None' = None, *, phase_encoding: 'str | None' = None, exponent: 'float | None' = None, b_ref: 'float | None' = None, edges: 'Sequence[float] | None' = None, fit_range: 'Sequence[float] | None' = None, zero_point: 'Sequence[float] | np.ndarray | None' = None, seed: 'int | None' = 12345, num_threads: 'int | None' = None, clip: 'str | bool | None' = 'both', margin: 'float' = 15.0) → 'None'[source]¶

    Bases: object

    The global eddy model (GEM): the eddy-current field of every volume, from their images, with one tensor.

    Each volume is added (add()) with its target (the image it should match once its field is undone), its sample, the head motion it is given and any field an earlier pass estimated; fit() then estimates the model, and mappings() gives each volume’s head motion and field. Without edges, the model is one EddyTensor (\(C\) and \(p\)); with them, a SegmentedEddyTensor, one \(C\) for each segment of b-values, with the exponent held. The segments share no parameter, so each is fitted on its own. With fit_range, the model is fitted on the volumes of a range of b-values only, and every volume added gets its field from it. Each volume’s shift along phase encoding is derived from its slopes and the fields’ zero point.

    The cost is the sum over volumes of ITK’s metric between the target and the sample, run in the stages of the settings (the itk section of AFFINE by default), and minimized by L-BFGS-B. A stage takes its metric, histogram bins, sampling, smoothing (in voxels), shrink factor and iterations from the settings; their step settings (learning_rate and the like) belong to ITK’s optimizer and do not apply. Both images of a stage are smoothed and then shrunk, and its points, drawn once, are the same for every volume. The images are prepared as ITK prepares them (clipped to the sample’s window, winsorized, histogram-matched as the settings ask), in their voxel frame, and kept within the mask’s box and margin; a point mapped beyond it does not count.

    Parameters:

    • align_config (Mapping or os.pathlike, optional) – ITK’s settings, or a file with an itk section (see get_settings()).

    • phase_encoding (str, optional) – The phase-encoding direction, as BIDS writes it.

    • exponent (float, optional) – Hold the exponent \(p\) at this value. Without edges, it is estimated by default; with them, it is held at DEFAULT_EXPONENT by default.

    • b_ref (float, optional) – The reference b-value, without edges (default: the geometric_mean() of the b-values). With them, each segment’s is the geometric mean of its b-values.

    • edges (Sequence of float, optional) – The b-values that split the segments, each with its own matrix \(C\) (see SegmentedEddyTensor); one model for all volumes if None.

    • fit_range (Sequence of float, optional) – Fit the model on the volumes whose b-value is in [low, high) only, and give every volume added its field from it (default: fit on all of them). With a single b-value in the range, the exponent is held: at exponent, or at DEFAULT_EXPONENT with a warning. Not with edges.

    • zero_point (ndarray, optional) – Where the fields are zero (RAS+, mm; default: the field_of_view_centre() of the volumes’ grid).

    • seed (int, optional) – Seed of the points drawn in each stage.

    • num_threads (int, optional) – ITK’s threads.

    • clip (str, optional) – Clip "sample", "predicted" (the target), "both" (default) or "none" of the images to the sample’s intensity window.

    • margin (float, optional) – How far (mm) beyond the mask’s box each volume’s images are kept.

    class nifreeze.registration.gem.SegmentedEddyTensor(self, edges: 'Any', parts: 'Any') → None[source]¶

    Bases: object

    Eddy-current tensors, one \(C\) for each segment of b-values, which give every volume its field.

    edges split the b-values into segments: segment \(k\) holds the b-values from edges[k - 1] up to, not including, edges[k] (the first, those below edges[0]; the last, those from edges[-1] on). Each segment has its own EddyTensor, so the slopes need not follow one power of the b-value: within a single shell, its tensor takes up the shell’s scale.

    Examples

    >>> tensor = SegmentedEddyTensor(
...     [1500.0],
...     [
...         EddyTensor(0.02 * np.eye(3), b_ref=1000.0),
...         EddyTensor(0.05 * np.eye(3), b_ref=2000.0),
...     ],
... )
>>> gradients = [[1.0, 0.0, 0.0, 1000.0], [1.0, 0.0, 0.0, 2000.0]]
>>> tensor.slopes(gradients).round(6).tolist()
[[0.02, 0.0, 0.0], [0.05, 0.0, 0.0]]
>>> tensor.segments([1000.0, 1500.0, 3000.0]).tolist()
[0, 1, 1]
