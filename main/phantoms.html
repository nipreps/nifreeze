    Synthetic reference data¶

    The sensitivity benchmarks compare model predictions on an aligned dataset with predictions on copies of it whose volumes moved or were distorted by known amounts. Real data cannot serve as the aligned reference: they carry motion and eddy-current distortions of their own, as large as the smallest perturbations the benchmarks add. NiFreeze therefore simulates its reference data with TRXScan (a port of MITK Fiberfox) from one phantom anatomy, under the acquisition protocols of real studies, and simulates every perturbation with its ground truth (#548).

    Acquisition protocols¶

    The protocols are chosen to span the axes along which models differ most, while standing for data that NiFreeze users actually process:

    • the number of shells and the largest b-value (one to four shells, b=1000 to 3500);

    • the number of directions per shell and the coverage of the sphere (32 to 150 directions; half or whole sphere, which decides whether eddy-current distortions cancel over the directions);

    • the voxel size and the signal-to-noise ratio it implies (1.25 to 2 mm);

    • prevalence: the protocols behind large public datasets.

    The minimal clinical acquisition

    tools/phantoms/protocols.toml records, for each protocol, its gradient table (tools/phantoms/protocols/<name>.b: one x y z b row per volume, unit vectors in world RAS coordinates as prescribed), the source of the table with its checksum, license and citation, the acquisition parameters the simulation needs, and the signal-to-noise ratio of the b=0 images. tools/phantoms/fetch_protocols.py rebuilds the tables from their sources, and its --check option compares them with the stored ones. tools/phantoms/measure_snr.py measures the signal-to-noise ratio on the real data of each protocol, where they are openly available: from 8.9 (Sherbrooke, at 1.5 T) to 19.4 (AOMIC-PIOP2). The values of HCP-YA (9.0) and ABCD (13.4), whose data are not openly available, are extrapolated from published or measured ones, as the catalog records.

    Sources come in different frames, all converted to world RAS coordinates. The b-vectors of HBN and AOMIC are converted with the header of the image they came with; those of ABCD, Sherbrooke and Stanford are taken in the voxel frame of an image aligned with the world axes (LAS for ABCD, as dcm2niix writes the axial images of the same Siemens sites). Diffusion contrast does not depend on the sign of a b-vector, so these assumptions only affect the sign of simulated eddy-current distortions. Three tables share one set of 64 directions, up to axis flips: those of HBN, Sherbrooke and the single-shell dataset ds000114, which is why the clinical protocol is AOMIC-PIOP2 instead of the latter.

    Generating the reference datasets¶

    tools/phantoms/phantom.py is the only code that imports TRXScan, pinned to one commit of its development branch (the motion fixes it needs are not released). It is a script with inline dependencies, which uv builds into an environment of its own:

    uv run tools/phantoms/phantom.py reference --protocol sherbrooke --out <dataset>

    simulates TRXScan’s adult phantom (subject sub-60501, 400,000 streamlines) under one protocol of the catalog: its gradient table, voxel size, matrix, phase-encoding axis and echo time (90 ms when the protocol does not document it). The acquisition is clean: no motion, eddy currents, susceptibility distortion or noise, and no T2 weighting along the readout, which would blur the images along phase encoding; the T2 contrast between tissues comes from their signal at the echo time instead. The protocol’s field of view is kept, so a short slab crops the phantom. Under <dataset>/sub-60501/dwi it writes:

    • *_rec-clean_dwi.nii.gz: the noise-free magnitudes (float32);

    • *_rec-noisy_dwi.nii.gz: one noisy realization, at the protocol’s signal-to-noise ratio, with complex Gaussian noise of standard deviation sigma = mean white-matter b=0 / SNR (stored as int16);

    • *_desc-brain_mask.nii.gz, *_dwi.b (the gradients) and *_dwi.json (the acquisition, the noise and the provenance);

    • *_desc-<map>_truth.nii.gz: TRXScan’s ground truth, the fiber peaks and the scalar maps its compartments imply (FA, MD, kurtosis, NODDI and others).

    The noise of each volume is drawn from a generator keyed on a seed and the volume’s index (nifreeze.testing.phantoms.add_noise()), so the analysis can add the same noise to any stored noise-free volume, in the reference or in a perturbed copy of it. uv run tools/phantoms/phantom.py verify checks, at a coarse resolution, that repeated runs are identical and that a subset of the gradient table reproduces the same volumes of the full run, which the perturbed copies rely on.

    Perturbed copies¶

    uv run tools/phantoms/phantom.py cells --protocol sherbrooke --out <dataset>

    simulates the perturbations of the reference, each with its exact ground truth. Only diffusion-weighted volumes are perturbed.

    Rigid motion

    For each seed and magnitude m (0.5, 1, 2 and 4 by default), every volume gets a rotation of m degrees about a random axis through the brain’s centroid and a translation of m mm in a random direction, drawn by perturbation_plan(). Each moved volume is simulated again from the phantom moved in the scanner, on the reference’s grid, so the anatomy turns relative to the gradients as in a real scan.

    Eddy currents

    For each magnitude e (2, 4 and 8 voxels by default), every volume is simulated with TRXScan’s linear eddy-current model, without decay over the readout, at the strength that displaces some voxel of the brain by e voxels along phase encoding. TRXScan applies one strength to every volume, so each volume is distorted in proportion to its b-value and its most distorted voxels lie at the edges of the brain; at the weakest magnitude, the typical volume moves some voxel by about one voxel. The displacement of each volume is known in closed form: a Distortion along phase encoding, with one term per voxel axis (TRXScan applies the b-vector’s world components to the grid axes as given).

    Both

    Every moved volume is also simulated with each eddy-current magnitude.

    Only the changed volumes are written, noise-free, under <dataset>/derivatives/perturbations/sub-60501/dwi, one file per set: desc-e<e> (every diffusion-weighted volume, with eddy currents of e voxels), desc-m<m>s<seed> (the moved volumes) and desc-m<m>s<seed>e<e> (the moved volumes with eddy currents), where 0.5 is written 0p5. The moved volumes come in the order of the plan, so a copy with K moved volumes takes the first K of them; the copy that moves one volume alone takes just that one. Each file has a sidecar with the index of each of its volumes in the reference (SourceVolumes), the motion matrices in world coordinates (Motion, mapping head positions before the motion to positions after it), and the eddy-current strength, coefficients and the acquisition time of each phase-encoding line (Eddy). A copy is assembled from the reference: the eddy-current volumes of magnitude e replace their counterparts, then the first K moved volumes replace theirs.

    verify also checks that the closed-form displacement reproduces TRXScan’s eddy-current images (within 10⁻⁴), and that a moved volume’s center of mass moves as its matrix says (within a fifth of a voxel). A moved volume takes about 35 s at 2 mm (about 50 s at 1.25 mm): the moved phantom is gridded once, then simulated without and with each of the three eddy-current magnitudes. The default grid of one protocol thus takes from about 1.5 hours (AOMIC-PIOP2) to 14 hours (HCP-YA).

    TRXScan’s signal is a sum of Gaussian compartments, as the tensor and kurtosis models assume, so these models may fit the synthetic data better than real ones.

    The dataset of continuous integration¶

    uv run tools/phantoms/phantom.py fixture --out <dataset>

    simulates a dataset small enough for every pull request (about 21 MB, under two minutes on a laptop): the Sherbrooke protocol at 4 mm with 50,000 streamlines, keeping its b=0 volume and 8 directions of each shell, spread over the sphere. It writes the reference and three perturbed copies, with the layout above: rigid motion of every diffusion-weighted volume at 1° and 1 mm (whose first quarter is a copy too), eddy currents of 2 voxels, and both. The volumes keep their order in the protocol; the sidecars list them (ProtocolVolumes), and the gradient file holds only their rows.

    In GitHub Actions, the composite action .github/actions/phantom-fixtures restores this dataset from the cache, or simulates it when the generator, its environment, the protocol or the NiFreeze code it calls have changed, and points the NIFREEZE_PHANTOMS environment variable to it. The generator’s environment is locked in tools/phantoms/phantom.py.lock (uv lock --script tools/phantoms/phantom.py), so that a dataset simulated again comes from the same versions of every package.

    Example: the Healthy Brain Network protocol¶

    The figures below show the HBN protocol (1.8 mm, two shells of 64 directions), drawn by tools/phantoms/figures.py from a dataset generated with phantom.py (the reference, and copies with 4° and 4 mm of motion and with eddy currents of 2, 4 and 8 voxels), next to a real HBN subject acquired with the same gradient table:
