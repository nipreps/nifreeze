    The eddy-current truth¶

    This experiment (#618) asks whether the displacement given in the sidecar of each eddy-current copy of the phantoms can serve as the truth for scoring eddy-current correction, at the phantoms’ native resolution. It also measures how closely a warp of the undistorted volume can imitate the volumes that TRXScan forms. It involves no registration.

    Important

    This report’s first version concluded that scoring a correction needs the sidecar’s coefficients divided by 1 - s. That holds only for a model that warps the reference onto the distorted grid, as this experiment’s fit does. NiFreeze corrects in the other direction, and the audit of The error audit (#626) shows, by the 99th percentile of the residuals, that its truth is the sidecar’s coefficients as they are. Consequences gives the corrected conclusions.

    The question¶

    Each eddy-current copy of a phantom (Synthetic reference data) comes with the displacement of every volume along phase encoding, in closed form (Distortion). The displacement is a polynomial of degree 1, d = c0 + c1 x + c2 y + c3 z. Here x, y and z are the coordinates along the voxel axes, measured from the field’s center and divided by its radius. In these phantoms the radius is the voxel size, so x, y and z count voxels from the center. The coefficients are in mm, and c0 is zero. TRXScan does not form the distorted volumes by moving voxels. It forms them as an EPI readout does: each phase-encoding line accrues the phase of the off-resonance field at the time the line is acquired. A warp works differently: it resamples a volume at displaced points. The warp model of this experiment forms each distorted volume by warping the undistorted one, so it maps each point of the distorted grid to a point of the undistorted volume. It also scales the intensity by the Jacobian of the displacement, so that no signal is created or lost. A correction by registration warps in the opposite direction: it samples the distorted volume at the points of the reference’s grid that the distortion moved (The error audit).

    Three questions were registered before the run:

    Q1, the readout model

    Does Distortion, forming each volume as the readout does from the sidecar’s coefficients, reproduce TRXScan’s volumes? This had been checked on the 4 mm phantom of continuous integration, within 10⁻⁴ of the volume’s maximum, but not at native resolution.

    Q2, the warp model

    How far from TRXScan’s volume is the undistorted volume, warped by the sidecar’s displacement? This gap was registered as the floor of any correction by warping.

    Q3, a fit without registration

    Which four coefficients bring the warped undistorted volume closest to TRXScan’s volume, by least squares? How far is their displacement from the sidecar’s?

    The registered decision rule is as follows. If the fit’s displacement is within 0.05 voxel of the sidecar’s on every fitted volume of every copy, the sidecar’s polynomial is the truth for scoring eddy currents. Within 0.05 voxel means that the 95th percentile of the difference over the brain is below 0.05 voxel. Otherwise, the fit is the truth, and the report says why.

    Data¶

    Both protocols of the phantom dataset are used, without noise: Sherbrooke, with voxels of 2 mm, and HCP-YA, with voxels of 1.25 mm. Phase encoding runs along the second voxel axis. Each distorted copy is compared with an undistorted counterpart:

    • The eddy-only copies e2, e4 and e8 are compared with the clean reference.

    • The combined copies m<m>s0e<e> are compared with the motion-only copy m<m>s0, which has the same motions. The combined copies cover motions of m = 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 (a rotation of m degrees and a translation of m mm), and eddy currents of e = 2, 4 and 8 voxels. TRXScan distorts each volume after moving it, in the scanner’s frame, so the motion-only volume is the undistorted one.

    Q1 and Q2 use every diffusion-weighted volume of the 15 copies. Q3 uses the development volumes of five copies (e2, e4, e8, m1s0e4 and m4s0e4). These are every third volume on Sherbrooke and every ninth on HCP-YA, starting from the first. On HCP-YA, every ninth volume misses the shell at b = 2000:

    How the errors are measured¶

    For Q1 and Q2, the model forms each volume from its undistorted counterpart. The warp model resamples with cubic B-splines. The formed volume is compared with TRXScan’s volume, voxel by voxel, within the brain mask. Each absolute difference is divided by the largest intensity of TRXScan’s volume. Each volume reports the largest of these residuals and their 99th percentile (p99).

    For Q3, the four coefficients of a volume are fitted by scipy.optimize.least_squares(), with the Levenberg-Marquardt method, starting from zero. The residuals are the differences between the warped undistorted volume and TRXScan’s volume, divided by its largest intensity. They are taken over the brain mask grown by as many voxels as the copy’s largest displacement, plus one, so that the fit also sees where the displaced signal lands. The error of the fit is the difference between its displacement and the sidecar’s, at every voxel of the brain, in voxels along phase encoding. Each volume reports the mean of that error, its 95th percentile (p95) and its largest value.

On every volume of every copy of both protocols, its largest residual within the brain is at most 7.3 × 10⁻⁶ of the volume’s maximum (the table below, row Readout, column Largest, worst). The same holds over the whole volume (check C of The error audit).

    Warped by the sidecar’s coefficients, the volumes are far from TRXScan’s (Q2). The largest residual within the brain has a median of 0.13 to 0.16 of the volume’s maximum, depending on the protocol and the magnitude.     What the results show¶

    The sidecar’s coefficients and the best warp’s differ by one factor per volume. Q1 shows that the sidecar describes TRXScan’s volumes, within 7.3 × 10⁻⁶ of the volume’s maximum, yet the warp that best matches them does not use the sidecar’s coefficients (Q3). Looking at the fitted coefficients after the run, each volume’s three fitted linear coefficients were the sidecar’s multiplied by one common factor, which changed from volume to volume, and the fitted constant term was near zero. In the volume shown above, for example, the sidecar’s coefficients are 0, -0.0802, -0.3556 and -0.0717 mm, and the fitted ones are -0.0021, -0.0681, -0.3017 and -0.0609 mm: the linear terms are about 0.85 times the sidecar’s.

    The fit estimates the inverse of the sidecar’s map. This explanation was posted on the issue (comment) before the second run, so the analysis that follows is post hoc. Let d be the sidecar’s displacement, and u the world direction of the phase-encoding voxel axis (direction).

    • The readout model maps each point p of the undistorted volume to where its signal appears. The signal from p accrues the phase of the field at p, so it appears at q = p - d(p) u. This is the sidecar’s map.

    • The warp model maps the other way, from each point q of the distorted grid back to the undistorted volume. The warped volume shows at each point q what lies at q + dw(q) u.

    For the warp to imitate the readout, its map must be the inverse of the readout’s: q + dw(q) u must be the source p of every point q, so dw(q) = d(p). Let s be the slope of d along phase encoding, in mm per mm, which is the same everywhere for a field of degree 1. Then d(p) = d(q) + s d(p), because p lies d(p) further along phase encoding than q. Solving gives dw(q) = d(q) / (1 - s). The warp that imitates the readout therefore has the sidecar’s coefficients divided by 1 - s: the coefficients of the inverse map. This report calls them the inverted coefficients. In the shown volume, s = -0.178, so the inverted coefficients are the sidecar’s times 1 / 1.178 = 0.849.

    The intensities match as well. The readout spreads the signal of a length along phase encoding over 1 - s times that length, so it scales the intensity by 1 / (1 - s). The warp scales it by its Jacobian, 1 + s / (1 - s), which is the same.

    Against the inverted coefficients, the fit is within the threshold on every volume (Q3b). The worst p95 of the fit’s error is 0.012 voxel on both protocols; measured from the sidecar’s coefficients, it was 1.4 voxels on Sherbrooke and 0.6 on HCP-YA (the fit table). The fitted linear coefficients differ from the inverted ones by at most 0.00069 mm on Sherbrooke and 0.00023 mm on HCP-YA, against up to 0.085 and 0.015 mm from the sidecar’s. The fit’s constant term, which neither the sidecar’s nor the inverted coefficients have, reaches 0.006 mm and 0.0017 mm. Q3b is not an independent test of the explanation: its fits are those of the first run, which suggested the explanation. It confirms on every fitted volume what the comment reported.

    The inverted warp still leaves a gap to TRXScan’s volumes (Q2b). Q2b is the comparison that the first run had not made. Inverting the coefficients removes most of the growth of the warp’s worst residual with the eddy currents: at 8 voxels, it drops from 0.91 to 0.32 on Sherbrooke, and from 0.57 to 0.38 on HCP-YA, close to its values at 2 voxels (0.29 and 0.36). The median of the largest residual barely changes, though: it is 0.13 to 0.14 at every magnitude, as at 2 voxels, where the two warps give nearly the same residuals. The median p99 is 0.021 to 0.027. These residuals are taken within the brain mask, while the distorted brain reaches beyond it along phase encoding. Within the mask grown by the largest displacement plus one voxel, the median of the largest residual over all the copies is 0.15 on both protocols, rather than 0.13 on Sherbrooke and 0.14 on HCP-YA, and its worst value is the same, 0.33 and 0.40 (check C of The error audit):

    That part of the gap comes neither from the displacement, which is now the right one, nor from the scaling of the intensity, which matches the readout’s (above). It is a difference between forming a volume by the readout and warping it. One explanation, not tested here, is that the readout forms the volume in k-space, line by line, while the warp interpolates with cubic B-splines; the two differ most where the phantom’s intensity changes sharply. The residuals in the figure of the inputs run in stripes along phase encoding, which is consistent with that explanation but does not test it.

    Consequences¶

    • The sidecar is the truth of how TRXScan formed the volumes (Q1). Its coefficients give the readout’s map, from each point of the reference to where its signal lands: x to x - d(x) u.

    • The truth for scoring a correction is the sidecar’s coefficients as they are. NiFreeze registers each volume (moving) to a prediction (fixed) on the reference’s grid, so its estimate maps each point of the reference into the distorted volume, as the sidecar’s map does. Sampling each distorted copy at x - d(x) u, with the Jacobian, reproduces the undistorted volume more closely than the inverted coefficients or the opposite sign do, by the 99th percentile of the residual (check B of The error audit). For these linear fields, no conversion is needed; for a field of higher degree, the readout’s map has the same form, but that is not tested. The inverted coefficients are the truth only for a model that warps the reference onto the distorted grid, as Q2 and the fit of Q3 do.

    • The registered rule compared maps that point in opposite directions. The fit estimates the inverse of the sidecar’s map, so its coefficients differ from the sidecar’s wherever the field changes along phase encoding. The rule failed for that reason, not because the sidecar is wrong.

    • The mapping on a combined copy has the opposite sign from the one registered. The phantom moves first, so a reference point x lands at y = M x in the scanner. The field acts at y, in the scanner’s frame: Q1 shows it on the combined copies, where the readout of the motion-only volume reproduces TRXScan’s. The readout then shows the point at T(x) = y - d(y) u. The issue had written y + d(y) u. The sign follows from the derivation above, which holds for every copy: with the opposite sign, the fitted coefficients would be the sidecar’s divided by -(1 + s), and they are not. Check B of the audit confirms both the sign and the map on every combined copy.

    • Q2 and Q2b measure how well a warp imitates the readout, not the floor of a correction. The registration called Q2’s gap the floor of a correction by warping. A correction samples the distorted volume rather than the undistorted one, so the corresponding residual for a correction is the one left by sampling each copy at the sidecar’s points, which check B of the audit measures. Neither is strictly a lower bound: a slightly different map might leave a smaller residual, as the fit of Q3 shows for the warp model.

    • The inverse of a displacement of degree 2 is not a polynomial of degree 2. That matters only where a warp imitates a distortion. A volume distorted by warping (Distortion without a readout), as planned to test the robustness of eddy-current models, has the inverse of its displacement as the truth of a correction, which must be computed point by point: along each phase-encoding line, that means solving a quadratic equation. A volume distorted through the readout keeps the polynomial itself as that truth.

    • The docstring of Distortion described only the warp model (#624). #625 corrects it.

    Open questions¶

    • What makes the gap that the inverted warp leaves (Q2b)? Warping with band-limited interpolation along phase encoding, instead of cubic B-splines, would show whether the interpolation accounts for it.

    • Does the residual a correction leaves bias a registration’s estimate of the displacement? The fit of Q3 is itself a least-squares match over the brain, and the gap of Q2b leaves it within 0.012 voxel of the inverted displacement (Q3b’s worst p95), with a constant term of up to 0.006 mm that the inverted displacement does not have. A registration matches the images in the other direction, with another metric and other sampling, so whether its estimate is biased as little is for the experiments that register eddy-current copies (E0b and E3a in #614).

    Deviations from the registration¶

    • Q2b and Q3b were added after the first run, as described above. They were posted on the issue before the second run.

    • The fit’s residuals are taken over the brain mask grown by the copy’s largest displacement plus one voxel, where the registration said within the mask. Both runs did so.

    • The registered rule, when it fails, makes the fit the truth. The fit estimates the inverse map, so corrections that map the reference into the distorted volume are scored against the sidecar’s coefficients as they are instead.

    • The registration called Q2’s gap the floor of a correction by warping. This report gives Q2’s and Q2b’s gaps as measures of how well a warp imitates the readout, and takes the floor of a correction from the audit.

    • The first version of this report concluded that scoring a correction needs the inverted coefficients. The audit (#626) showed that this holds only in the warp model’s direction, and this version corrects it.

    • The registered mapping on combined copies had the opposite sign (Consequences).

    • The runs used 12 processes, where the registration planned six, on the same CPUs: six physical cores and their hyper-threaded siblings. The volumes of one copy at a time were spread over the processes.

    • HCP-YA needed more memory than registered: its largest process alone held 20.9 GB (the table of the runs below), where the registration expected the run to stay under 12 GB.
